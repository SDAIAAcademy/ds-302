How packs are scored

The rubric

Design and power
25%
Analysis
25%
Monitoring and guardrails
20%
Recommendation
20%
Reproducibility
10%
Every number reruns. A number the board cannot regenerate from the repository scores as a claim, not a result.

Full marks, briefly. Design and power: a pre-registration written before looking at the test columns, a power calculation on the pre-period, a written rule. Analysis: adjusted and unadjusted intervals with the sample-size gain. Monitoring and guardrails: a sequential plan with its false-positive rate measured, and a harm guardrail judged on a bound. Recommendation: Lab 4.3's page, decision first. Reproducibility: a clean clone reruns it.

Format

Ten minutes: seven to present, three for the board

1 · The change and its risk

Who it is for, what could go wrong, the harm guardrail. One slide.

2 · The design

Unit, metrics, limits, power, the sequential plan, the rule.

3 · The result and the decision

The effect with its interval, the guardrails, launch, iterate or stop.

Peer feedback. After each defence, everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.

Stop the presenter at seven minutes; what was not said is not scored. The three minutes of questions are the review-board defence, 20% of the course result.

What the board asks

One live change, then two questions

The live change

The assessor changes the traffic (for example, halves it) or the pre-period (for example, 14 days in place of 28). The participant reruns the power calculation and says what can still be detected and what they would change.

  • "Your harm rate is lower in treatment. Is that evidence the harm is gone?"
  • "Why this spending function, not a fixed horizon?"
  • "Your raw interval includes zero and the adjusted one does not. Which do you report?"
  • "What would make you stop this test on day 10?"

Use one live change for everyone so the defences are comparable: halving the traffic works for all three changes, and so does a 14-day pre-period, which lowers the correlation the adjustment relies on. The starter takes --customers, --pre-days and --seed. For the adjusted-versus-raw question, the expected answer is that the pre-registered estimator decides, and both are reported.
