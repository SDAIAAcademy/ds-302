Review of Day 3

Which design measures the courier-priority rule as it would run for everyone?

Joud wants the effect of giving priority to orders under a new dispatch rule, as it would be if every order in a city used it. Customer randomisation showed a 10.4-minute gain. Which design answers her question?

Customer randomisation with twice the traffic
Order randomisation within each customer
A city-by-hour switchback, analysed over blocks
Customer randomisation with a Bonferroni correction
Why. In a block every order runs the same rule, so the couriers are not shared between arms and the difference between blocks estimates the global effect.

Answer: C. The simulator's global effect was 1.8 minutes; the switchback recovered about 1.5 with its weighting. Remind the room that the switchback pays in power.

What changes when the treatment is a model

An AI feature brings three guardrails a button never needed

Latency

A model answers in seconds, not milliseconds; the tail is where customers leave.

Cost

Every call is paid for; cost per conversation is a metric with a limit.

Harm

A fluent answer can be wrong: an ingredient that is not there, an allergen missed.

National reference. The SDAIA Generative AI Guidelines list misinformation among the risks and ask for monitoring and human supervision of a deployed system.

The guidelines (Government and Public editions, course sources) describe risks including misinformation and inaccurate output, and expect monitoring. A harm guardrail in a pre-registration is how a test turns that expectation into a number with a limit. Ask: which of your AI features has a harm you could count?

Lab 4.1 · the case

T-401: Luqma replaces the help menu for half of Sufrah's customers

  • 60,203 customers with Luqma, 59,797 with the old help menu, four weeks.
  • Primary: orders per customer over the four weeks.
  • Guardrails: p95 response time at most 5 s, cost per conversation at most 0.12 SAR, complaints at most +1.0 per 1,000 orders.
  • Harm guardrail: wrong ingredient or allergen complaints at most +0.3 per 1,000 orders, judged on the upper bound.

The written rule

Stop if the harm guardrail fails. Otherwise launch if the primary interval is above zero and every guardrail holds; iterate if a non-harm guardrail fails.

The rule was written before the test, which is the point: nobody can argue the harm away after seeing a lift. Numbers on the next slides are from the solution run of Lab 4.1, full mode (120,000 customers).

Checkpoint

The harm estimate is small. Is that enough?

A new AI menu search raises allergen complaints by +0.10 per 1,000 orders, 95% interval -0.20 to +0.40. The pre-registered limit is +0.30 on the upper bound. What does the rule say?

It holds: the difference is not significantly above zero
It fails: the upper bound, +0.40, is above the limit
It holds: the estimate, +0.10, is below the limit
It cannot be judged without a longer test
Why. The test must show the harm is below the limit. An interval that reaches +0.40 has not shown that; a larger test might.

Answer: B. Connect to non-inferiority from Day 1, session 3: the same logic, applied to harm.

Lab 4.2 · the case

Twenty cuisine segments, four of them "significant"

  • T-205 added photos to menus. Overall: a small lift.
  • The team cut the result by the cuisine of each customer's favourite restaurant: 20 segments.
  • Below 0.05: Mandi (p = 0.00001), Coffee (0.001), Indian (0.040, a drop), Desserts (0.049).
64%
of 20-test analyses with no effect anywhere show at least one p below 0.05; 1.0 false win on average

The 64% is from Lab 4.2's simulation, 20,000 sets of twenty tests, and matches 1 - 0.95²⁰ because the segments are independent. Ask: in your team's dashboards, how many cuts does a typical result get?

Two error rates to control

Control the chance of any false win, or the share of false wins you report

Family-wise error: Bonferroni, Holm

The chance of even one false win stays at 5%. Bonferroni tests each p against 0.05 / 20 = 0.0025; Holm steps down from the smallest p and never rejects less.

False discovery rate: Benjamini-Hochberg

Among the segments you report, the expected share of false ones stays at 5%. Sort the p-values; keep the largest k with p(k) ≤ 0.05 k / 20.

On the platforms. GrowthBook's documentation offers Holm-Bonferroni and Benjamini-Hochberg corrections for many metrics or many variations.

Use FWER when one false claim is costly, as in a launch claim; FDR when you are exploring a list and can accept a stated share of false leads. A single pre-registered primary metric needs no correction.

Checkpoint

A segment with p = 0.01

Ruba cut a test into 20 city-and-platform segments. One, Abha on Android, has p = 0.01; the other nineteen are above 0.2. With Benjamini-Hochberg at 5%, what does she report?

A win in Abha on Android, because 0.01 is below 0.05
A win with a 1% chance of being false
That segment analysis is never allowed
No segment survives: the smallest p must be below 0.05 / 20 = 0.0025
Why. For the smallest p-value, Benjamini-Hochberg's threshold is 0.05 × 1 / 20; 0.01 is above it, so nothing is reported.

Answer: D. The rank-1 threshold of Benjamini-Hochberg equals Bonferroni's; the procedures differ only from rank 2 onward.

AI-tutor lab. Participants write Benjamini-Hochberg by hand and the lab compares it with statsmodels; the common bug is keeping only the ranks that pass rather than all ranks up to the largest that passes. The segment table is given, so the lab starts on its own.

The review board

Read for faults before you read the result

  1. Did the split work? Sample ratio (Lab 1.2).
  2. Is the analysis the pre-registered one, at the planned time? (Labs 1.4, 3.1)
  3. Is the effect settled, or fading? (Lab 4.1)
  4. Did every guardrail hold, the harm guardrail above all? (Labs 1.3, 4.1)
  5. Was a segment or a metric picked after the fact? (Lab 4.2)
Why this order. A fault early in the list makes every later number suspect.

Each item is a lab of this course. Five write-ups, W1 to W5, all end "we recommend launch"; each has one fault or none. The board's own log is in data/reference, opened only after the participant has decided.
